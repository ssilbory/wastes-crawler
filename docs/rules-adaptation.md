# Rules adaptation

WWN SRD is the source of truth for every mechanic it covers; this document only defines how tabletop concepts become computer rules, plus the house rules. Exact numbers come from the SRD, never from memory or D&D habits.

**Adopted from the SRD:** attributes, skills, backgrounds, classes and foci, saving throws, 2d6 skill checks, attacks and Shock, HP, System Strain, healing, encumbrance, equipment, High Magic and Arts, monster stats, reaction rolls, morale, Instinct checks, dungeon exploration time, and hirelings.

**Initial class scope:** Warrior, Expert, Mage (High Mage and Elementalist traditions), and Adventurer partial-class combinations. Other traditions (Necromancer, Healer, and so on) come after the core loop works.

**Class switching (house rule):** a character can switch to a new class only in town, and only after reaching 4th level in their current class. XP requirements follow overall character level, so switching doesn't reset the XP track; new levels go into the current class. Each level grants the attack bonus and hit die of the class it was taken in. Progress toward a class's next bonus stays with that class when the character switches away. Switching back to an earlier class is allowed and picks up where the character left off, and there is no level cap. The first level in a new class grants that class's full level-1 row: its hit die, its attack bonus, its focus picks, and its class abilities. Total attack bonus is the sum of each class's table value at that class's own level. Characters keep everything from earlier classes. A character with more than one Effort-using class has one Effort pool, calculated from the best skill and the best attribute any of their classes allows; the skill and attribute can come from different classes.

**Progression past 10th level (house rule):** classes keep following their normal progression, gaining hit points, attack bonus, foci, and the like at the same per-level rate as before 10th, without repeating the early bump of levels 1 and 2. Concretely, every class and casting table cycles the gains of levels 3 to 10: level 11 gains what level 3 gained over level 2, level 18 gains what level 10 gained over level 9, and level 19 starts the cycle again. Maximum spell level stays at 5. XP follows the SRD Fast table to 10th level (93 XP); each later step is 3 larger than the one before, so 11th level is at 117 XP and 12th at 144. Save targets never drop below 2+. See docs/decisions/0001-high-level-progression.md.

**Foci:** SRD 1.6.0 applies: a new character gets one focus of any kind, Warriors and Partial Warriors another combat focus, and Experts and Partial Experts another non-combat focus. A second pick of the same focus adds its level 2, and only a focus's first level grants its bonus skill, which counts as a creation skill pick (SRD 1.2.2).

At character creation the game applies focus bonus skills, Die Hard's extra hit points, and Developed Attribute's +1 modifier (to saves and hit points too). Focus benefits that change sheet numbers apply from Phase 3d: Armsmaster, Deadeye, Unarmed Combatant, Shocking Assault, Close Combatant, Impervious Defense, War-bred, Horned, and Four-legged; Armsmaster's benefits skip unarmed and natural attacks, as its text says. Every other focus benefit is recorded on the character and takes effect when the system it touches arrives: combat in Phase 3f, travel and camping in Phase 5.

**Gear:** a new character takes a starting package or rolls 3d6 × 10 crowns to buy SRD gear (SRD 1.7.7). One crown is one silver piece, so SRD prices apply as listed and copper prices come to tenths of a crown. Packages are sized for the character: small armor for Goblins and Gnomes, and matching barding for a centaur's worn armor at no extra cost. The packages are in data/content/starting-packages.json.

| Rule            | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Attack lines    | One line per Readied weapon and mode: melee, thrown (the better of Stab and Shoot), or ranged (Shoot), plus natural weapons and an unarmed attack. Shock applies only in melee |
| Overloaded load | Items past the Readied and Stowed limits add up; up to half Strength over is Overloaded, and more can't be carried                                                             |
| Worn items      | One suit of armor, one barding, and one shield; clothing marked § is free while worn. Worn armor and shields count as Readied                                                  |

**Magic:** new characters may take a full High Mage or Elementalist, either one as a partial class beside a Partial Warrior or Partial Expert, or both as dual partial casters (SRD 4.3.0). The class list, with each class's traditions and casting tables, is in data/content/class-options.json.

| Rule             | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Magic skill      | Each tradition grants Magic as a creation skill pick (SRD 1.5.10, 1.5.7), so a dual caster starts at Magic-1. A pick that would pass level-1 is replaced by another skill (SRD 1.2.2)                                                                |
| Effort           | 1 + the best Effort skill + the best Effort attribute modifier across every casting class held, Developed Attribute included. One lower, but at least 1, only when every casting class is partial. Full casters have no floor, as the SRD gives none |
| Arts             | Each class picks its own tradition's arts from its level-1 Arts Gained (SRD 4.1.0); Elementalists also get Elemental Resilience and Elemental Sparks                                                                                                 |
| Starting spells  | Four first-level spells for full and dual casters, two for partial ones (SRD 1.7.5), from every list the traditions can learn. The player prepares up to the casting table's limit                                                                   |
| Casting in armor | Any worn armor, even a war shirt, or a shield blocks casting. Armored Magic 1 allows armor of encumbrance 2 or less and a shield; level 2 allows any armor. Barding counts at its doubled encumbrance                                                |

**Party and formation:** six player characters plus two NPC slots, arranged in two rows of four. Quest NPCs, rescued NPCs, and hirelings occupy the NPC slots. When both are full, a new NPC can join only after one is dismissed. Centaurs take one slot like anyone else, but large NPCs such as giants and dragons take two. NPCs can occupy any slot, front or rear. Some quests replace a PC's slot with an NPC; a PC out of the party can rejoin at any tavern. Each rear slot is shielded by the front slot directly ahead of it, so a gap in the front row exposes whoever stands behind it.

An empty front slot exposes the rear slot behind it:

```
            +-------------------------------------+
            |             Enemy melee             |
            +-------------------------------------+
                                              |
Front row   [ PC 1 ]  [ PC 2 ]  [ PC 3 ]  [ empty ]
                                              v
Back row    [ PC 4 ]  [ PC 5 ]  [ PC 6 ]  [ Rescued NPC ]
             shielded by the slot in front  exposed: no one in front
```

With three in front and four behind, the fourth rear slot has no cover and can be hit in melee.

**Formation rules:**

| Rule              | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shielding         | A rear slot is shielded by the front slot directly ahead of it. An empty front slot exposes the rear slot behind it to all melee. A two-slot NPC, such as a giant, takes two adjacent slots in the same row and shields both slots behind it                                                                                                             |
| Melee             | Only the front row can make melee attacks, on both sides                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Reach             | Long spears, halberds, and giant creatures can make melee attacks from the second row, at disadvantage. This is the rear-cover disadvantage, not an extra step                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Flying            | Flying creatures can attack any slot, front or rear, from any row, without the rear-cover disadvantage. They never get cover themselves                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Ranged and spells | Can target any slot, front or rear                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Rear cover        | Attacks against or made from a rear slot have disadvantage, and rear-row characters have advantage on saves. Slings and grenade-style thrown attacks made from a rear slot avoid the disadvantage. An exposed rear slot has no cover: attacks against it and its saves are normal, but its own attacks still have disadvantage, with the same exceptions |
| Front drop        | At the end of the round, a front-row character at 0 HP moves to an empty rear slot, preferring the one directly behind them. A downed character in an exposed rear slot is a valid target, but enemies only target them when no other target is available                                                                                                |
| Promotion         | If no rear slot is empty, the rear character with the highest HP is promoted to the vacated front slot, and the downed character takes their rear slot. NPCs are promoted and demoted like PCs. HP ties and other edge cases, such as every rear character being down, are decided at random                                                             |
| Moving rows       | Moving between rows takes an action in combat                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Positioning and range  | Two rows of four slots, governed by the formation rules above. Enemies stand in up to three rows of up to eight creatures each. An encounter that needs more than three rows is rerolled. | GM rulings             | Explicit rules written in `docs/rules-adaptation.md`, with a difficulty table for every check type                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Initiative and actions | SRD initiative; each character acts on their turn from a command menu. Characters can have a default action, and auto-combat resolves the whole fight with no display and applies the results                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Reaction rolls         | Rolled for every intelligent encounter. Result drives hostile, wary, neutral, or friendly options                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Parley                 | Skill check menu (talk, bribe, intimidate) when reaction allows it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Morale                 | Checked per enemy row. A row that fails flees or surrenders. | Instinct checks        | Monster AI hook. **Combat engine (Phase 3f):** these interpretations apply in the engine and the `pnpm sim` simulator.

| Rule                 | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Initiative           | Side initiative: each side rolls 1d8 once per fight, the party adding its best Dex modifier and best Alert bonus. The party wins ties. Alert 2 characters act before both sides. Within a side, characters act front row first, left to right                                                                                                                                                       |
| Monster weapons      | Monsters whose damage is "Wpn" use a real SRD weapon assigned in data/content/monster-combat.json, for its damage, Shock, reach, and ranged mode. Monster tags there mark flying, reach, unliving (immune to sleep), and metal (a target for Stunning Shock)                                                                                                                                        |
| Combat magic         | Only a subset of spells and arts is usable in combat for now, listed with its effect in data/content/combat-magic.json: The Coruscating Coffin, The Wind of the Final Repose, Damnation of the Sense, Wardpact Invocation, Ineluctable Shackles of Volition, Elemental Blast, Pavis of Elements, Stunning Shock, Empowered Sorcery, and Inexorable Effect. Monster casters attack with weapons only |
| Monster tactics      | A monster attacks the reachable target most likely to drop: the lowest HP after its expected damage, ties at random. A monster acting on Instinct attacks a random reachable target instead                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Instinct             | Each foe rolls Instinct (1d10 at or under the score) at the start of round 2, and again after its row holds a Morale check. A failed check lasts until the end of that round                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Morale               | Each enemy row checks Morale (2d6 over the row's best score fails) on its first loss, and every row checks once when half the enemy side is down. A row that fails flees. Surrender comes later                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Victory              | The party wins when no foe can act. Sleeping and enthralled foes count as defeated                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Dying                | A PC at 0 HP is dying and dies at the end of the sixth round after the round they fell. Stabilizing is a Heal check (Dex or Int) against 8 + rounds since the fall, +2 without a healer's pouch, once per dying character per round. A stabilized PC is Frail and dies if dropped to 0 HP again. Monsters die at 0 HP. After a win, the party keeps tending the dying until each lives or dies      |
| Warrior abilities    | Killing Blow adds half the Warrior's level, rounded up, to damage and Shock. Veteran's Luck is used defensively: once per fight, it turns a hit that would drop the Warrior into a miss                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Shields and defense  | A shield ignores the first melee Shock each round. Total Defense gives +2 AC and immunity to Shock until the character's next turn                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Auto-combat defaults | PCs stabilize the dying when not in melee, then cast (sleep on a row of two or more, Coffin on the toughest, Shackles, Stunning Shock on metal, Elemental Blast on the weakest), then attack the weakest foe with their best usable attack. Pavis of Elements is cast as an On Turn action                                                                                                          |
| Default actions      | Each saved character has a default action: Auto (the auto-combat defaults above), Attack weakest (the best usable attack on the weakest foe), Total Defense, or Repeat last command. A default that can't apply this turn, such as repeating an attack on a foe who has fallen, falls back to Auto. Auto-resolve plays the rest of the fight on everyone's defaults                                 |
| Monster vs monster   | Both sides stand in monster rows of up to eight, three rows deep, and both check Morale and Instinct. The party formation, shielding, and front drops apply only to a side of characters                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Arena fights         | Exhibitions: a fight never changes the saved characters. Each fight starts from the characters as saved, so every rematch starts fresh                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Deferred             | Explore (no hidden sites yet), water and fuel, the encumbrance of the rations die and loot dice, and surrender                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

**House rules (our text, inspired by Year Zero Engine):**

| House rule               | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Resource dice            | Torches, lanterns, rations, and ammo are tracked as a die (d12, d10, d8, d6, d4). Roll on use: rations once per day for the party, torches and lanterns every exploration turn and after each combat (a fresh torch is a d6, a pint of oil fills a lantern to d12), and ammo once per combat. Lantern oil restores a lantern to its full die. A 1 or 2 steps the die down; stepping below d4 means it's gone |
| Encumbrance              | A character can carry Stowed items worth encumbrance points up to their Strength score, and Readied items up to half their Strength. Each loot die, ammo die, and torch or lantern die is one Stowed item, and a flask of lantern oil is half an item. Weapons, armor, shields, and a lit torch or lantern are Readied                                                                                       |
| Overloaded               | A character can carry up to half their Strength more, but must make a Physical save on entering each hex or every 20 dungeon turns. Each failure costs 1 System Strain                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Darkness                 | No light source: attack penalty, automap off, higher encounter chance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Advantage / disadvantage | On attacks and saves, advantage adds 1d6 and disadvantage subtracts 1d6. On 2d6 skill checks, each step instead rolls an extra d6: keep the highest two for advantage, the lowest two for disadvantage. Sources stack and cancel one for one, capped at two steps either way                                                                                                                                 |
| Push                     | Reroll a failed skill check for +1 System Strain, once per check. If the push breaks the character, Broken applies from the next roll. A Broken character can't push. Reaching maximum strain through pushing or through the Hungry, Sleepless, and Cold conditions makes the character Broken. Strain also caps magical healing, so pushing spends the same budget                                          |
| Strain casting           | A caster can cast a known spell without a spell slot by taking System Strain equal to the spell's level. As with pushing, reaching maximum strain this way makes the character Broken, and a Broken character can't strain-cast                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Broken                   | Disadvantage on attacks, saves, and skill checks until a full rest in town, and no more pushing or strain casting. While Broken, magical healing, potions, poisons, rare monster attacks, and the Hungry, Sleepless, and Cold conditions can push strain past maximum; at double maximum strain the character dies                                                                                           |
| Rest economy             | A full rest happens only in town and gives full recovery. Elsewhere, each quarter day of rest recovers 1 System Strain. Dungeon camping costs rations and risks an encounter. System Strain caps magical healing to prevent rest-spam                                                                                                                                                                        |

**System Strain outcomes:** maximum strain is the character's Constitution score. This combines SRD 2.5.2 with the Push, Strain casting, Broken, and Rest economy rules above. Reaching the maximum from any source makes the character Broken.

| Situation                                                                                     | Result                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Push or strain casting, not Broken                                                            | Allowed only if strain after the cost stays within the maximum                                                                 |
| Magical healing, first aid, or a potion, not Broken                                           | Refused if it would pass the maximum; no healing happens                                                                       |
| Condition (Hungry, Sleepless, Cold) or failed Overloaded save, not Broken                     | Adds strain up to the maximum                                                                                                  |
| Poison or a monster attack, not Broken                                                        | Adds strain; if that would pass the maximum, strain stops at the maximum and the character is knocked out for at least an hour |
| Healing, a potion, poison, a monster attack, a condition, or a failed Overloaded save, Broken | Adds strain past the maximum; at double the maximum the character dies                                                         |
| Push or strain casting, Broken                                                                | Refused                                                                                                                        |
| Rest                                                                                          | Recovers 1 strain per quarter day of rest, none while Hungry, Sleepless, or Cold. Broken stays until a full rest in town       |
| Full rest in town                                                                             | Strain goes to 0 and Broken clears                                                                                             |

**Progression and economy (house rules):**

| Rule               | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| XP                 | Awarded for quests, and as mostly flat XP per fight scaled by difficulty against the party. Harder fights give more; easy fights give little or none. Difficulty comes from running the fight through the auto-combat simulation with the party itself, as it stands, against the enemy side. XP is set from how often a PC goes down in the simulated fights. The simulation runs in a Web Worker, started in the background when combat starts, with its own RNG stream so it never advances the game's RNG |
| Loot dice          | Fights and treasure award loot as dice, such as 2d6, carried unrolled. Each loot die is one Stowed item, so 2d6 takes two                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Loot exchange      | In town, loot dice are rolled and converted into that many crowns                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Spending threshold | Each town absorbs a set amount of crown spending without issue. Past it, prices rise smoothly at half the overage: `price × (1 + 0.5 × overage)`, so 20% over means +10%. 25% of crowns spent with craftsmen count toward this threshold                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Loot threshold     | Half the spending threshold. Past it, loot payout drops smoothly at twice the overage: `payout × max(0, 1 − 2 × overage)`, so 10% over pays 20% less and 50% over pays nothing. Craftsmen keep a separate trade pool that doesn't count against this threshold                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Resets             | Both thresholds reset on the first visit to a town and after one month                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Economic climate   | On reaching a new town, or after more than a month of game time, the party's crowns are adjusted by a random amount from −30% to +30% for the current economic climate                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

**Overland in Phase 5 (interpretations):** how the engine reads the travel rules above.
