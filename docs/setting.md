# The Dark Lands: Setting Bible

Oct 3, 2026 · @Samuel Silbory

## Premise

The player runs a party of goblins, orcs, elves, gnomes and beastmen in the Dark Lands, 20 years after the Dark Lord vanished. The former slave races are digging out of the rubble and building settlements; the human paladins and rangers are now the raiders. Play splits between hex travel across the wasteland and dungeon crawls through the ruins of a 1000-year empire.

The inhabitants call their land the Dark Lands or the Continent, and many have no concept of anywhere else. What lies beyond the sea is rumor at best.

Years are counted back from the present day of play.

| 40–20                      | An alliance from a distant continent invades; decades-long war                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 20                         | Alliance seers can no longer detect the Dark Lord's will. Believing one of their plans worked, and with their homeland economies and governments failing, the alliance goes home. Over the next few years the Dark Lands realize he is gone |

| 10–0                       | The dark races dig out of the rubble and found settlements, under threat from flesh-crafted monsters, dark warlords, and rangers and paladins raiding from shrinking strongholds and the coast, supplied by factions of the former alliance |

### The Dark Lord's fate

Nobody knows what happened to him. The alliance ran hundreds of plans through numerous groups in secret, and all it knows is that one day its seers could no longer detect his will in the world; it assumes one plan worked, but not which.

Orcs and goblins remain in theory bound to him, so the other races fear he could return or someone could take his place. Some believe his bound agents carry secret orders to avenge or restore him.

## Peoples

No PCs are human. The playable races are the Dark Lord's former slaves, and they split into two moral categories: orcs and goblins had no choice, most elves and gnomes did. The exception is the Dark Lord's best agents of any race, who were bound to his will. The outside world distrusts elves and gnomes for the wrong reasons but isn't entirely wrong, and fears orcs and goblins for reasons that are mostly no longer true. The dark races share no common identity.

### Elves and gnomes

The only large communities of elves and gnomes in the world are in the Dark Lands. The elf homeland was an island to the northeast, and the gnomes had kingdoms on several continents; all fell to the Dark Lord's forces over 500 years, and no ruins of either race exist in the Dark Lands.

Both races are ageless, so Elders are hard to tell from the young, and Elders often pretend to be younger than they are. They are the only living memory of the homelands.

Elves and gnomes often held high positions in the Dark Lord's forces. For centuries most people assumed they were his agents, and they weren't always wrong.

Service spectrum, each a source of NPCs and quests:

| Service type  | Then                                                 | Now                                                                 |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bound         | One of his best agents; could not disobey            | Possibly still executing orders; looks identical to a true believer |
| Coerced       | Served under threat to family held in the Dark Lands | Some still don't know they're free                                  |
| Opportunist   | Took the deal for status and comfort                 | Rewriting their past, or selling to warlords                        |
| True believer | Genuinely loyal                                      | Core of any loyalist faction; know where things are buried          |
| Saboteur      | Undermined from inside                               | Unprovable; nobody believes them                                    |
| Agent abroad  | Spy or infiltrator in other lands                    | Stranded, or still active as a sleeper                              |

Specific roles: elves as overseers, scribes and flesh-crafter assistants; gnomes as forge and machine artificers who can repair or repurpose the Dark Lord's devices. Orcs and goblins who served under elf and gnome officers remember who did so willingly.

### Beastmen

Beastmen gestate for 4–6 months and mature 4–6 months after birth. Most were made from herd animals for loyalty, some from pack animals, and a few from predators, which were set loose behind enemy lines. They were rarely bound, which is why so many could desert.

Beastmen are essentially child soldiers who know no other life, and that is what keeps a bought beastman loyal. Nobody expects them to live long enough to grow independent; most do by age 5, but few live that long. Their natural lifespan is unknown, since until recently few lived past 10 before dying in battle or being culled. There are rumors of ancient beastmen, but no one knows whether they exist or whether they are standard beastmen.

Every beastman has a service record: an army, a unit, a commander, and a reason for deserting. Old units or warlords may hunt them, and as a finite population they depend on the flesh-crafting they escaped for any future at all.

Source animal as a racial option: herd (common, loyal), pack (less common), predator (rare, with an infiltrator or feral past). Free beastmen split over the crafters: seize the knowledge to make free beastmen, destroy it, or accept extinction.

## The binding and standing orders

All orcs and goblins remain, in theory, bound to the Dark Lord's will, and the older generation still carries out his last orders 20 years after he vanished. That is why the other races fear them: if he returns, or someone takes his place, every orc and goblin could be his again. No one knows what it would take to command them. The alliance would like to see both races die out.

Beastmen were rarely bound; they were made from herd animals for loyalty instead.

This gives orc and goblin PCs a generational wound the other races don't share: a parent may still be guarding a gate they were ordered to guard, and can't be talked off it. Elder elves and gnomes have the opposite burden: free will, memory, and regrets.

### Bound agents

The Dark Lord could also bind people. His best agents among humans, elves, gnomes and other races were bound to his will and still are, so some of the elves and gnomes the world called his agents truly were, with no more choice than an orc. Binding can't be reliably detected and is never broken.

He didn't micromanage his agents; most acted on their own judgment within his will. Some believe they carry secret orders to avenge or restore him.

Hooks:

- Bound human agents may still sit inside the former alliance nations, or in the stronghold garrisons.
- To outsiders, and to their own people, a bound Elder looks exactly like a willing collaborator.
- Bound agents are the most capable holders of standing orders: not a goblin guarding a gate, but a spymaster still running a network.
- The rumored secret orders are the setting's paranoia engine: any Elder, officer or stranger might be carrying one.
- With no reliable test and no cure, accusation is cheap and proof is nearly impossible; any Elder can be suspected and none can be cleared.

Standing-order encounters:

| Standing order             | Encounter                                                   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Guard this fortress        | Defenders who can't leave, even starving                    |
| Maintain the forge or mine | Labor gangs producing for nobody                            |
| Patrol this road           | Checkpoints demanding passwords nobody remembers            |
| Escort prisoners to X      | A column marching to a destroyed city                       |
| Hold until relieved        | Garrisons waiting for relief that will never come           |
| Hunt the alliance          | Bound hunters still tracking humans, possibly useful to PCs |

Non-lethal resolution: completing the order, finding the countersign, or presenting proper authority ends a garrison's standing order without a fight; the binding itself remains. Released NPCs feed the rescue and NPC-slot systems.

Command authority is the central MacGuffin: the other races fear someone could take the Dark Lord's place over the bound, and no one knows what that would take. Rumored means include an heir, a seal, regalia, or a lieutenant's residual authority, and every warlord and loyalist chases them, because it means an obedient army of every orc and goblin alive.

## Flesh-crafting and the beastman trade

Flesh crafters made the orcs and goblins as the Dark Lord's slaves, and were constantly experimenting to produce a replacement for the current breed. The experiments produced several improved orc and goblin breeds, the older breeds were actively culled, and the failures were horrible monsters. That history is why orcs and goblins especially want crafters dead.

Training is the only scarce input, so a warlord's power is roughly the number of crafters they hold. Beastmen are born from animal mothers; making them takes goats or other animals, certain herbs, some arcane ability, and the training.

| Input          | Scarcity    | Why                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Training       | Scarce      | Crafters are hunted (orcs and goblins especially want them dead), hoard what they know, and warlords never let theirs leave |
| Animals        | Common      | Goats and other livestock                                                                                                   |
| Herbs          | Available   | Specific herbs required                                                                                                     |
| Arcane ability | Some needed | Limits who can be trained                                                                                                   |

Beastmen are still being made today. Freelance crafters sell beastman slaves; others work for warlords and would-be warlords.

Crafter situations:

| Situation         | Status                                        | Hook                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Warlord-held      | Captive in practice, privileged in appearance | Extraction or assassination                          |
| Freelance         | Hidden, mobile, paranoid                      | Tracking, bounties, protection rackets               |
| Retired in hiding | Living under a false identity                 | Exposure in a settlement; hunted by orcs and goblins |
| Apprentice        | Partial knowledge, desperate for the rest     | Easiest to recruit or turn                           |

Other consequences:

- Production is a strategic clock: a warlord's pregnant herd becomes an army 8–12 months later, and hitting the herd before the births is a different mission from fighting the troops after.
- A warlord's troop mix shows which herds they hold; predator stock signals plans for raids behind enemy lines.
- Breeding labs hold records of which orc and goblin breed is which and who was culled, which matters enormously to orcs and goblins; failed experiments may still lurk in them.
- A crafter's written notes are the most dangerous loot in the game; burning, selling or keeping them is a party decision every faction cares about.
- Rescuing a captive crafter weakens one warlord but releases the knowledge back into the world.
- A rescued crafter is the most volatile possible NPC-slot companion: orc and goblin PCs want them dead, beastman PCs have complicated feelings, elves may have done the same work.
- Settlements may quietly make or buy beastman guards, which puts them at odds with the free beastmen they shelter.

## Threats and factions today

The settlements are squeezed from every side: monsters in the wilds, warlords building beastman armies, and human raiders from the strongholds and the coast.

| Faction                             | Status                                                                           | Role in play                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dark-race settlements               | Newly founded, fragile                                                           | Hubs, shops, mission givers, rescue targets                                            |
| Flesh-crafted monsters              | Made by the Dark Lord's crafters, including failed breeding experiments; roaming | Wandering encounters, lairs                                                            |
| Dark warlords and would-be warlords | Hold captive crafters, build beastman armies                                     | Bosses, extortion, fortress dungeons, unreliable patrons                               |
| Freelance crafters and slavers      | Sell beastman slaves                                                             | Caravans, markets, bounties                                                            |
| Inland human strongholds            | Alliance fanatics who stayed; ever-shrinking holdouts                            | Raiders, siege targets, prisoner rescues                                               |
| Coastal humans                      | Raid from the coast                                                              | Hit-and-run raids, beachheads                                                          |
| Former alliance factions            | Overseas; send supplies and men to keep the Dark Lands from rising               | Distant backers of the raiders; want orcs and goblins extinct                          |
| Bound elder orcs and goblins        | Still executing the Dark Lord's orders                                           | Guardians, patrols, garrisons                                                          |
| Bound agents                        | Humans, elves, gnomes and others; still bound, acting on their own judgment      | Sleepers, infiltrators, tragic bosses; may hold secret orders to avenge or restore him |
| Loyalists                           | Waiting for the Dark Lord's return                                               | Cultists, recurring enemy, hunt for command authority                                  |

The fanatics may treat elves and gnomes differently from orcs and goblins: "rescue" and trial instead of extermination. To an elf PC, a rescue raid is a kidnapping.

## Mysteries and open questions

These are unanswered by design. Clues can surface in play, but the setting never resolves them.

- What happened to the Dark Lord: which plan, if any, worked? Is he dead, gone, or hidden from the seers?
- Which former alliance factions still send men and supplies, and do they agree on anything beyond keeping the Dark Lands down?
- What would it take to command the bound orcs and goblins?
- Do ancient beastmen exist, and if so, are they standard beastmen or something else?
- Do bound agents really hold secret orders to avenge or restore him, and what are they?
